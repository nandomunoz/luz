# @luz-ds/ai

## 0.1.0

### Minor Changes

- 90e1b13: First release of `@luz-ds/ai`: the `luz` skill (`/luz`) for AI assistants. `npx @luz-ds/ai init --target claude` (or `codex`, `gemini`, `cursor`, `agents-md`) puts the skill where your assistant reads it. The skill asks what you want to make with LUZ, writes a starter page for a landing page, a blog, an app or a documentation site (plain HTML or Astro) that `@luz-ds/check` reads without a finding (it does not test accessibility), and finishes it with you, writing nothing until you say go.

  The one executable, `luz-ai`, also lists and adds the five agents of luz-ai, for Claude Code, Gemini CLI and Codex, with the fields you give it filled in: `luz-agent-accessibility`, `luz-agent-ux` (design and UX), `luz-agent-seogeo` (SEO and GEO) and `luz-agent-legal` (legal and privacy in the European Union, not legal advice), which read and propose, and `luz-agent-optimiza` (speed and Core Web Vitals), which edits files after you agree to its changes and is not written for Gemini CLI yet; adds six small skills of one task each, `luz-readme` (the README of the repository, in plain, specific language without the stock phrases of machine-written text), `luz-theme` (a brand theme over the tokens), `luz-migrate` (moving a site onto LUZ step by step), `luz-translate` (a translation a person still reviews), `luz-meta` (the head and the SEO files) and `luz-license` (the MIT licence and third-party notices; it asks once whether you hold the rights to publish under MIT, and writes nothing if you do not or are not sure), with `luz-ai skills`, `luz-ai add <name>`, `luz-ai list` (what the project has) and `luz-ai remove <name>` (takes one piece out, only the files luz-ai wrote); `luz-ai init --all` installs the skill, the agents and the skills together, and `/luz magic <description>` builds a whole prototype site from one prompt, with no question, into a new folder; checks the front matter of skills and agents; and diagnoses a project. What it writes keeps the MIT notice of LUZ (a `LICENSE` in the folder of each skill, an `SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT` header comment in each agent, and a shorter comment in the starter page that says the content you add is yours), and the README that `luz-readme` writes ends with one line that says an AI drafted it. It makes no network request, runs no program, installs nothing, and never writes over a file or through a symbolic link unless you say so.
