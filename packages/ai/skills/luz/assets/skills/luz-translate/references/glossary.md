# The glossary and the register

Ask for both before you write a line. They are the person's, and a translation without them drifts from page to page.

## The glossary

A table the person corrects before you start:

| Source term | Translation | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the term as it appears | what it becomes, or "keep" | where it appears, or the other meaning to avoid |

- Build it from the terms that recur in the pages: product and feature names, words of the interface (a button, a menu), terms of the field.
- Names of products, of code, of commands and of files are kept in the source language unless the person says otherwise.
- A term with two meanings gets one row for each, with the note that tells them apart.
- If the project already has a glossary (a file, a table in its docs), read it and use it. It wins over yours.
- Use the glossary to the end of the work. If you find a term it lacks, add it to the report, do not decide in silence.

## The register

Ask which one, once, and keep it:

- **Spanish:** `tú` or `usted`; Spain or Latin America for the vocabulary; whether the text is gender-neutral.
- **Arabic:** Modern Standard Arabic is the usual choice for a site; a dialect only if the person asks. Say that a native reader must read it.
- **Other languages:** formal or informal, and the variant (for example the country).

## Figures and separators

Every figure of the source appears in the translation: numbers, versions, dates, prices. Write them with the separators of the language (a decimal comma in Spanish, for example). Do not turn a number into a word or the reverse, and do not round it.
